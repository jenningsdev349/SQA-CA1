# Team Reflection

## Adam

I'm happy with how this assignment turned out. The project plan was followed by all team members which contributed to the consistent style and quality of work we produced. Pull Requests were done to a high standard, everyone made valuable insights in the comments on top of pointing out smaller errors within files. Writing my own article - Task Estimation - and contributing to the Code Reviews article was the easiest part of the assignment in my opinion. There was plenty of material online to source and the project plan was helpful in laying out the format that the articles followed. I took on the brunt of "admin" type work - setting up the repo, creating the project plan document, creating the article introductions document. This was difficult but I had support from the other contributors on these tasks so the workload was made easier. There were some small errors that occurred in the project, the three of us created branch names with slightly varying names for example. This was not a big deal but in a wider team context ideally branch names should all follow the exact same naming convention. Some comment chains that occurred could have also been handled in a more efficient way offline, which would have helped articles get merged faster and avoid some of the questions posed in comment form. We all followed a similar template for PRs but a more rigid template should have been laid out better in the project plan.

## Liam

I think this project turned out good and I am satisfied with the part I played in it. I was the primary researcher and writer for the Code Review article, the secondary researcher and writer for the Code Styling article, and a PR reviewer within the group workflow. That gave me a good mix of responsibilities and made me look at the handbook from more than one angle instead of only focusing on one section. The Code Review article was the part I had the most ownership over, so that is where I put most of my effort. The research itself was fairly manageable because there was a lot of material available, but the harder part was turning that research into something that actually matched the purpose and format of the handbook. My role in the Code Styling article was smaller, but it still mattered because it involved taking a well-structured article and building upon it in a way that the article still felt consistent with the rest of the project. I also think arranging meetings early to discuss roles helped the group stay organised. Having clear ownership over articles made the workload easier to manage and reduced confusion about who was responsible for what. On top of that, being careful about how we split the files and branches helped avoid unnecessary merge conflicts, which kept the project moving more smoothly. One improvement I would make to the process next time would be introducing a more fixed article template from the start. We had general guidance, but a stronger template for headings, formatting, and section structure would have made it easier to keep the handbook feeling like one whole product, rather than three seperate articles.

## Hamzah

Overall, I'm happy with how the project went. The work was completed to a high standard, and the PRs were a pain-free process thanks to the guidelines laid out in the project plan - everyone knew exactly what they had to do, and when - and the feedback given in the PRs genuinely improved the articles afterward. Writing the articles was mostly pain-free, the guidelines were sufficient but I felt like they could have been more detailed and specific. I had work cut out for me in standardising the formatting across the handbook to keep consistency because I feel like a standardised template for an article could have been used instead of general bullet points on formatting - something along the lines of a 'template.md' file to refer to would be the first thing I would consider proposing for next time.

## Overall Feelings

### What Went Well

The team is happy with the standard of work produced for this project. The work was structured well enough that there was little hassle involved in completing tasks because: 

* #### Clear roles were assigned and work was divided among team members early in the project
* #### Team members could rely on each other for support on tasks outside of their assigned roles, if needed
* #### Good guidelines set for creating PRs streamlined the process of merging branches

### Even Better If

There were minor issues in the project that stemmed from having a style guide in the project plan that was too generalised, resulting in more work required to standardise the format across the handbook - and some comment chains on PRs that could have been easily addressed and resolved outside of long comment chains to decrease the time taken to merge a branch.

### Measures To Take For Next Time

1. #### Create a more rigid style guide in the planning phase in the form of a concrete template to refer to, naming conventions for branches, etc
2. #### In the PR guidelines, account for scenarios where issues may be resolved outside of PR comments by declaring a rule of thumb to follow - i.e if there is an issue with phrasing contained only within a single line, considering trying to resolve it outside of PR comments first
